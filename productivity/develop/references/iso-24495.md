# ISO 24495 Plain Language Standard

**Goal:** Your intended readers should be able to **find what they need, understand what they find, and use that information**.

### 1. Relevant — Do readers get what they need?

* Identify the intended audience.
* Understand their goals, tasks, and context.
* Consider their knowledge and familiarity with the topic.
* Include information readers need to achieve their purpose.
* Remove unnecessary or distracting information.
* Put the most important information first or where readers expect it.
* Make required actions and decisions clear.

### 2. Findable — Can readers find it easily?

* Organize information in a logical order.
* Use descriptive headings and subheadings.
* Divide long information into manageable sections.
* Use lists, tables, or other structures where they improve scanning.
* Keep related information together.
* Use consistent terminology and navigation.
* Make important information visually easy to identify.

### 3. Understandable — Can readers understand it?

* Use familiar, everyday words where possible.
* Explain necessary technical or specialized terms.
* Prefer concrete and precise wording.
* Keep sentences reasonably short and focused.
* Express one main idea at a time.
* Make relationships between ideas explicit.
* Prefer direct constructions and active voice where appropriate.
* Avoid unnecessary nominalizations, jargon, abbreviations, and complexity.
* Use examples when they make difficult concepts easier to understand.
* Make numbers, dates, conditions, and requirements unambiguous.

### 4. Usable — Can readers act on it?

* Clearly state what readers should do next.
* Present instructions in the order they should be performed.
* Make responsibilities and deadlines explicit.
* Explain consequences where readers need them to make decisions.
* Design the document for its actual medium: print, web, mobile, form, etc.
* Check that layout and formatting support the task.
* Test important documents with representative users where possible.
* Revise the content based on testing or feedback.

**Rule of thumb:** Plain language is not simply *shorter or simpler writing*. It is communication designed around the reader and their purpose.
